We now turn to the study: of fluids in motion. In general the
motion of a fluid in the presence of obstacles is complicated. Under
certain conditions, the flow of the fluid may become turbulent, and
fluid elements may move along very complex paths. In order to discuss
these complexities, we would resort to the Euler and Lagrange coordinate
schemes. The Lagrange method involves the specification of the coordi- ~
nates of a fluid particle as a function of time and some initial coordi-
| nate values. This is a direct generalization of the methods of ordinary
mechanics. In the Euler method we specify the density and velocity at
each point of the fluid as a function of position and time. We will
make some use of these ideas in ane considerations of fluids, but in
order to avoid the complications discussed above, we first make some
simplifying assumptions.
